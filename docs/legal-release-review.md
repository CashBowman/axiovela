# Legal review before distribution

The MIT License already contains a broad warranty disclaimer and limitation of liability. Preserve its exact text and copyright notice in source and installers. The app now exposes that license and BETA_NOTICE.md from Help. The versioned beta notice adds explicit warranty and liability language for maintainers, AI output, data loss, third-party charges, voluntary assistance, and research validation. It is a supplemental notice, not a replacement license or a claim that liability has been eliminated. The [Mac handoff guide](mac-installation.md) includes a pre-download acknowledgment template; receipt or acknowledgment alone does not establish an enforceable contract.

No license can promise absolute immunity. For example, California Civil Code section 1668 restricts contractual attempts to disclaim responsibility for fraud, willful injury, or violations of law. Enforceability and any mandatory consumer rights depend on the jurisdiction, conduct, and distribution arrangement. Have a software lawyer review the release, any beta agreement and assent mechanism, and whether an entity or insurance would be appropriate before public distribution. Do not advertise “no liability” as an established fact.

The review should cover ownership of all contributed code and assets (including any employer/university rights if applicable), dependency notices, trademark clearance for the final name and logo, claims about scientific validity, provider/compute billing, privacy and data transfers, and how testers obtain support or report security problems. The beta notice describes current behavior; update it if telemetry, hosted storage, automatic uploads, billing, or third-party integrations change.

A repository name being available does not establish trademark rights. Search related marks and services before committing to a brand. A new SVG drawing provides a source trail but does not prove global uniqueness or trademark clearance.

## First-wave review, 2026-09-07

The current MIT license retains its standard permission and disclaimer text. The existing beta notice already covers AI errors, host tool access, data loss, charges, third-party data transfers, voluntary participation, backups, and non-waivable rights. No additional waiver language was added during this review: more expansive wording cannot establish immunity. Source and documentation checks do not establish legal enforceability or ownership.

Outstanding factual/legal checks belong to the maintainer and, where needed, counsel: rights to contributed code/assets and any university/employer claims; trademark clearance; whether the actual distribution and support arrangement requires additional terms or privacy disclosures in applicable jurisdictions. These are unresolved, not certified by automated tests. Keep risk disclosures available before download and retain the exact release materials. Acknowledgment records, if collected, should remain private and contain only necessary information.

Unsigned distribution is disclosed as an intentional limitation. Do not describe GitHub hosting, checksums, successful tests, or the MIT license as Apple verification, a security certification, a guarantee of reliability, or protection from all claims.
