# Scientific integrity

Axiovela helps researchers connect hypotheses, experiments, analysis, and writing. The researcher remains responsible for the design, interpretation, and published claims.

Generated prose is not evidence. A claim should be traceable to saved runs, measured metrics, artifacts, and verified citations. Assistant instructions encourage these practices; they do not certify that every output is correct.

- State the hypothesis, baseline, primary outcome, data split, and assumptions before interpreting the result.
- Save the code, parameters, random seed, environment details, run status, metrics, and logs needed to inspect or rerun the experiment.
- Distinguish observation from interpretation. Include sample sizes, uncertainty where supported, and limits on generalization.
- Keep figure values, axes, labels, captions, and manuscript numbers consistent with the recorded measurements.
- Verify references against their source. Do not invent citations, measurements, significance, or proofs.
- Treat failures and negative results as part of the research record. Review edits to figures, summaries, and manuscripts after rerunning an experiment.

The [included example](../examples/linear-regression/README.md) demonstrates these conventions using synthetic data. Its measured result illustrates a workflow, not a new scientific finding or validation of Axiovela's AI capabilities.
